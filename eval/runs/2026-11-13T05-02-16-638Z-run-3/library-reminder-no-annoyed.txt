- **Cause insufficiency.** Borrowers know the due date in advance [know-date]: the reminder only informs borrowers if the library has working contact details and the borrower actually reads the message.
- **Additional cause.** Borrowers know the due date in advance [know-date]: the date stamped on the checkout receipt already tells borrowers the due date, so the reminder may add little beyond jogging their memory.
- **Clarity.** Borrowers know the due date in advance [know-date]: the reminder's real effect is that borrowers remember the due date close to it, not that they know it in advance, which they already did at checkout.
- **Cause insufficiency.** More books come back on time [on-time]: knowing the date and being able to reach the library are not enough; borrowers must also have finished with the book or be willing to return it unfinished.
- **Possible negative branch.** Send a reminder two days before each due date [reminder]: frequent reminders cost staff time or messaging fees, and borrowers may see them as spam and start ignoring all library messages.
